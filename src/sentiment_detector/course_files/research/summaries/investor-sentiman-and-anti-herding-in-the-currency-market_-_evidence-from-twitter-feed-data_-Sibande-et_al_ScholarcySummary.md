# [Investor Sentiment and (Anti) Herding in the Currency Market: Evidence from Twitter Feed Data](https://doi.org/10.1080/15427560.2021.1917579)

## [[Xolani Sibande]]; [[Rangan Gupta]]; [[Rıza Demirer]] et al

## Abstract

This paper establishes a direct link between (anti) herding behavior in currency markets and investor sentiment, proxied by a social media-based investor happiness index built on Twitter feed data. Our analysis of daily data for nine developed market currencies suggests that the foreign exchange market is generally characterized by strong anti-herding behavior. Utilizing the quantile-on-quantile (QQ) approach, developed by Sim and Zhou (Citation2015), we show that the relationship between investor sentiment and anti-herding is, in fact, regime-specific, with anti-herding behavior particularly prominent during states of extreme investor sentiment. The effect of sentiment on anti-herding is generally stronger during extreme bullish sentiment, while average sentiment is associated with less severe anti-herding. The findings lend support to behavioral factors in asset pricing models and suggest that real-time investor sentiment signals can be used to monitor potential speculative activity in the currency market.

### Methodology

The study uses a sample of nine developed-market currencies and calculates two dispersion metrics: the cross-sectional standard deviation (CSSD) and the cross-sectional absolute standard deviation (CSAD).
The study also estimates herding coefficients using OLS with robust standard errors and implements one-day rolling regressions with 250-day and 500-day windows.
Additionally, the study uses quantile regression to capture the dynamic dependencies in the data.

The results of the herding tests and the relationship between herding coefficients and the happiness index are also presented.

### Data Characteristics

The data exhibit leptokurtic distributions with high kurtosis and mixed skewness, indicating that the series are not normally distributed.
The ARCH-LM and Ljung-Box tests reveal the presence of heteroskedasticity and serial correlation in the data.
The unconditional correlations between currency pairs range from -0.83 to 0.82, with some pairs showing low correlation.

### Sentiment And Herding

The quantile-on-quantile regression analysis shows that the relationship between investor sentiment and anti-herding is regime-specific.
During periods of extreme bullish or bearish sentiment, anti-herding behavior is stronger, whereas average sentiment is associated with weaker anti-herding.
The findings suggest that real-time investor sentiment signals can be used to monitor potential speculative activities in the currency market.

## Differs from previous work

- During periods of extreme bullishness, we observe a positive effect of happiness, which means extreme bullish sentiment generally makes anti-herding even stronger. This can also be seen during extreme bearish sentiment when the effect becomes strongly positive. [^Daviou_2014_a]) argue that investors do not necessarily lose confidence during extreme increases in risk and instead, they argue, investors build confidence over sharp, subsequent declines in risk.

## Confirmation of earlier findings

- The effect of sentiment on anti-herding is generally stronger in extreme bullish sentiment states, while average sentiment is associated with less severe anti-herding. While the findings are generally in line with the evidence in the literature (e.g., [^Gavriilidis_et+al_2016_a]; [^Blasco_et+al_2018_a]) that links herding behavior to extreme events, they also lend support to the behavioral factors for asset pricing models and suggest that real-time investor sentiment signals can be utilized to monitor potential speculative activities in the currency market.

## Counterpoint to earlier claims

- The possible presence of herding behavior in the currency market is further motivated by the evidence of commonalities in currency traders’ risk preferences (e.g., [^Dominguez_1993_a]; Carlson 1998; Bensaid and De Bandt 2000; [^Mende_et+al_2004_a]; [^Osler_2005_a]; Bjønnes and Rime 2005) and the recent evidence that links the time- variation in risk preferences to currency excess returns ([^Demirer_2021_a]) and the profitability of carry trades ([^Demirer_et+al_2020_a]). Interestingly, although several studies have examined herding in the foreign exchange market, they remain limited in volume and in the use of empirical measures to detect herding (see [^Kim_et+al_2004_a]; [^Park_2011_a]; [^Pierdzioch_et+al_2012_a]; and others).

## Limitations

- The study notes that the literature on herding in the currency market remains limited in volume and the utilization of empirical measures to detect herding. The study also acknowledges that the drivers of herding in foreign exchange markets remain a subject of debate.
- The study notes that standard quantile regressions are limited in their ability to capture dependency in its entirety. The study also notes that financial markets are often characterized by regime-dependent patterns for normal, bullish, and bearish market states.
- The study does not explicitly discuss its limitations, but it notes that the choice of bandwidth in kernel regression remains uncertain. The study also uses a specific investor sentiment index based on Twitter feed data, which may not be representative of all investors.

## Future work

- The study suggests that future research could explore the relationship between herding and investor sentiment in other financial markets, such as equity markets. The study also highlights the importance of considering the dynamic nature of herding behavior in the currency market.
- Future work can build on the study's results by examining how real-time sentiment proxies drive intraday trades in these markets.
