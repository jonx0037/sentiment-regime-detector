# [AI and Machine Learning Models in Cross-Asset Class Investment Risk Analysis: A Case Study of Real Estate and Equities Markets](https://doi.org/10.1109/icssas66150.2025.11081061)

## [[Shaikh Sarfarazurrehman]]; [[Vijay Mane]]; [[Arpit Doshi]]

## Abstract
This research explores the application of MeanVariance Optimization (MVO) and advanced AI and Machine Learning (ML) models in constructing efficient investment portfolios that balance return and risk across real estate and equities markets. ==Through a comprehensive review of existing literature, we identify key gaps in traditional portfolio optimization methods, such as their sensitivity to input data and limited adaptability to dynamic market conditions==. This review underscores the importance of integrating AI-driven techniques to address these limitations, offering improved predictive accuracy, risk management, and sustainability in investment strategies. Our study analyzes historical data from 2010 to 2023 to evaluate the impact of MVO and AI models on portfolio performance, emphasizing strategies like diversification, regular rebalancing, and the integration of ESG factors for responsible investing. ==The results demonstrate that AI models, such as Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, significantly outperform traditional methods, achieving up to 29.52% cumulative returns and a Sharpe ratio of 0.98==. These findings highlight the transformative potential of AI in cross-asset investment risk analysis while emphasizing the need for regulatory safeguards to ensure market stability.

## Summary

### Introduction To AI In Finance
The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) into financial markets has revolutionized investment risk analysis and portfolio management.
Traditional methods like Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO) have limitations, such as sensitivity to input data and limited adaptability to dynamic market conditions.
AI-driven techniques can address these limitations, offering improved predictive accuracy, risk management, and sustainability in investment strategies.

### AI Models And Portfolio Optimization
AI models, such as Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, can significantly outperform traditional methods.
These models can evaluate the interconnections among asset returns and volatility for equities, real estate, and bonds.
The results demonstrate that AI models can achieve up to 29.52% cumulative returns and a Sharpe ratio of 0.98.
AI-driven strategies, such as AlphaOptimizerNet and transformer-based models, can also improve risk-return optimization and asset pricing.

### Regulatory Considerations
While AI and ML hold huge potential to revolutionize cross-asset investment risk analysis, major problems remain, such as data quality concerns, overfitting, and computational complexity.
Regulators must address these risks through measures like circuit breakers and transparency standards.
The development of fair and stable markets requires careful consideration of feature engineering, model selection, and ethical issues.
Quantum computing, federated learning, and Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) integration are also expected to shape the future of AI-driven financial markets.

### Performance
The optimization of cross-asset portfolios using AI and machine learning has been shown to be successful in investment risk analysis.
DRL, LSTM networks, and other transformer-based models have been proven to perform well in terms of portfolio risk management and returns, leveraging compared to traditional methods.
The mean annualized return of LSTM-based asset allocation models was 10.07% with a Sharpe ratio of 0.98, outperforming conventional passive strategies.
AI-driven models, such as IMCA-DRL, achieved a 29.52% cumulative return and a Sharpe ratio of 0.83.

### Risk Management
Portfolios need to be risk-managed, considering how asset classes are interconnected.
Real estate investment trusts (REITs) are found to be pervasive long-term volatility transmitters due to strong linkages with underlying assets.
The evidence reveals that shocks in REITs lasted longer than those in equities, commodities, and bonds, reaffirming their role in market destabilization.
Adaptive volatility forecasting should be incorporated into AI-driven models to mitigate exposure to cross-market risk.

### Regulatory Implications
The application of AI-based trading strategies increased market prediction accuracy, trading efficiency, and portfolio liquidity.
However, there may be problems, including increased market volatility, flash crashes, and ethical issues involving data imbalance and algorithmic biases.
Transparency in the use of AI models should be a main focus of regulatory frameworks, using explainable AI techniques to enable interpretability in the models.
Policymakers should institute circuit breakers and ensure the stability and efficiency of financial markets with regulatory safeguards.
