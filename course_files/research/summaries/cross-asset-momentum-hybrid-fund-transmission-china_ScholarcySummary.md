# [Cross-asset momentum and the hybrid fund transmission mechanism in China’s stock and bond markets](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0300781)

## [[Xiaowei Wang]]; [[Rui Wang]]; [[Yichun Zhang]]

## Abstract

The allocation of assets across different markets is a crucial element of investment strategy. In this regard, stocks and bonds are two significant assets that form the backbone of multi-asset allocation. Among publicly offered funds (in China, these correspond to mutual funds in the United States, with different names and details regarding legal form and sales channels), the stock-bond hybrid fund offers returns while minimizing risk by capitalizing on flows between the stock and bond markets. Our research on China’s financial market data from 2006 to 2022 reveals a cross-asset momentum between the stock and bond markets. We find that momentum in the stock market negatively influences bond market returns, while momentum in the bond market positively influences stock market returns. Portfolios that exploit cross-asset momentum exhibit excess returns that other asset-pricing factors cannot explain. Our analysis reveals that hybrid funds play an intermediary role in the transmission mechanism of cross-asset momentum. We observe that the more flexible the fund's asset allocation, the more crucial the intermediary role it plays. Hence, encouraging the development of hybrid funds and relaxing restrictions on asset allocation ratios could improve liquidity and pricing efficiency. These findings have significant implications for investors seeking to optimize their asset allocation across different markets and for policymakers seeking to enhance the efficiency of China’s financial market.

## Summary

### Introduction

China's financial market has experienced significant growth, with the total market capitalization of the A-share market reaching approximately 12 trillion US dollars and the bond market expanding to 20 trillion US dollars.
The link between the stock and bond markets has strengthened, leading to a more favorable interaction and facilitating optimal capital allocation.
Publicly offered funds, particularly hybrid funds, play a crucial role in China's financial market, with the total number of funds surpassing 10,000 and an overall size exceeding 3.7 trillion US dollars.

### Asset Allocation

The allocation of assets across different markets is a crucial element of investment strategy.
Investors face the critical issue of selecting an asset allocation strategy, with tactical and strategic asset allocation concepts derived from studies by Brinson et al (1991) and Ibbotson &amp; Kaplan (2000).
Hybrid funds balance returns and risk through investments in the stock and bond markets, and their growth in number and size demonstrates the evolution of China's investor preferences toward a more balanced, diversified asset allocation strategy.

Portfolios that exploit cross-asset momentum exhibit excess returns that other asset-pricing factors cannot explain.

Hybrid funds play a pivotal role in transmitting cross-asset momentum, with asset allocation ratios that reflect the extent of their intermediary role.
The study uses the CSI 300 Index and the SSE Corporate Bond Index to represent the stock and bond market indexes, and employs OLS regression and quantile regression models to investigate the cross-asset momentum effect.

### Momentum Effect

The momentum effect is a long-term influential pricing factor, with cross-sectional momentum referring to the phenomenon that historical winners continue to outperform historical losers, and time-series momentum referring to the persistence of an asset's returns over time.
Time-series momentum challenges the efficient market hypothesis, which holds that investors cannot profit from historical information.
The momentum effect in China's stock market is challenging to sustain in the medium- and long-term and is characterized by short-term reversals.

### China&#39;s Market

China's financial market exhibits unique characteristics, including a large number of individual investors, relatively high transaction costs, and short-selling restrictions.
The performance of the momentum effect in the market differs from that observed in developed countries.
The weak momentum effect in China's stock market is attributed to several factors, including restrictions on short selling and high transaction costs.
Institutional investors in China's stock market tend to adopt positive feedback trading strategies, while individual investors exhibit more random trading behavior.

### Correlation

The future return of the SSE Corporate Bond Index is positively correlated with its historical returns over the past 1 to 3 months.
The correlation duration between the future return of the SSE Corporate Bond Index and the historical returns of the CSI 300 Index is longer than that between the future return of the SSE Corporate Bond Index and its own historical returns.

### Momentum

The historical cumulative returns of the CSI 300 Index and the SSE Corporate Bond Index have limited predictive power for their future returns.
However, there is a cross-asset momentum effect, in which the historical returns of one index influence the future returns of the other.
The CSI 300 Index exerts a negative cross-asset momentum effect on the SSE Corporate Bond Index, while the SSE Corporate Bond Index has a positive cross-asset momentum effect on the CSI 300 Index.

### Portfolio Performance

The cross-asset momentum portfolio generates significantly positive excess returns, regardless of whether short selling is allowed or prohibited.
The economic and statistical significance of the excess returns is higher for the cross-asset momentum portfolio than for the single-asset momentum portfolio.
Incorporating the cross-asset momentum into the single-asset momentum strategy can significantly enhance the portfolio's performance.

### Intermediary Effect

The four hybrid fund indexes exhibit a significant intermediary effect in transmitting momentum between the stock and bond markets.
The historical CSI 300 Index rose steadily, while the hybrid fund indexes exacerbated declines in the SSE Corporate Bond Index.
For every 1% increase in the monthly return of the CSI Mixed Fund Index, Wind Partial Stock Fund Index, and Wind Flexible Fund Index, the monthly return of the CSI 300 Index decreases by approximately 0.074% to 0.084%.
The Wind Flexible Fund Index, with the most flexible asset allocation, is strongest at amplifying declines.

### Momentum Transmission

The findings suggest that the cross-asset momentum portfolio exhibits a significantly positive excess return, regardless of whether short selling is allowed or prohibited.
The excess return of the cross-asset momentum portfolio is economically and statistically significantly superior to that of the single-asset momentum portfolio.
The cross-asset momentum portfolio exhibits better robustness in terms of risk-adjusted performance compared to the single-asset momentum portfolio.

### Robustness Check

The results are robust to different holding and lookback periods.
The cross-asset momentum portfolio exhibits significant excess returns when the holding period is 1 month and the lookback period is 3, 6, and 9 months.
The findings are consistent under the condition that short selling is prohibited.
The influence of hybrid funds on the bond market has increased in recent years, and the connectivity from the stock market to the bond market is more pronounced than from the bond market to the stock market.

### Findings

The study reveals a significant cross-asset momentum effect between China's stock and bond markets, with a negative correlation between the stock market's historical momentum and the bond market's future return, and a positive correlation between the bond market's historical momentum and the stock market's future return.
The excess return of the cross-asset momentum portfolio outperforms that of the single-asset momentum portfolio.
Hybrid funds play an intermediary role in the cross-asset momentum transmission mechanism, amplifying the decline in bond prices when the stock market exhibits upward momentum and amplifying the rise in stock prices when the bond market exhibits upward momentum.

### Implications

The study's findings carry important policy implications for emerging markets.
Relaxing restrictions on the fund's asset allocation ratio and expanding its investment scope could boost the fund industry's vitality and promote its high-quality development.
Encouraging the development of the hybrid fund could improve the efficiency of capital and information flows between the stock and bond markets, reduce the impact of single-market risk, and better meet investors' evolving wealth management needs.
