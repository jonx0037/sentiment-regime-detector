# [Sentiment spillover and price dynamics: Information flow in the cryptocurrency and stock market](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2022.126983)

## [[Rocco Caferra]]

## Abstract

This study examines the sentiment–returns relationship in both the stock (S&P 500) and cryptocurrency (Bitcoin) markets. An explorative wavelet analysis evidences a period of episodic interconnectedness across different data frequencies. Therefore, Transfer Entropy (ET) measures remark the relative statistical significance, frequently outperforming traditional (VAR) estimates. In particular, ET methods successfully identify the mediating role of sentiments in connecting the two different markets. Hence, it is discussed how the potential cryptocurrencies' indirect linkage to the real economy operates through market sentiment.

## Summary

### Sentiment

The study examines the sentiment–return relationship in both the stock (S&P 500) and cryptocurrency (Bitcoin) markets.
Sentiment flows from different economic and financial spheres and impacts investors' mood and preferences in related markets.
The general impact of information and sentiment flows in financial markets is considered a potential mediating variable linking the two spheres.

### Methodology

The analysis consists of several steps that merge methodologies, including wavelet analysis, traditional linear causality (VAR) models, and entropy-based measures such as mutual information and Rényi Transfer entropy.
The wavelet coherence approach analyzes co-movement between time series in both the time and frequency domains, while comparisons across frequencies help identify synchronization at different scales.

### Results

The wavelet coherence analysis shows a general disconnection between sentiment metrics and financial returns series, with some market-specific sentiments–returns connections observed at lower frequencies.
The study finds that entropy-based methods, such as Transfer Entropy, successfully identify the mediating role of sentiments in connecting the two different markets, and that the potential indirect linkage between cryptocurrencies and the stock market passes through sentiment spillovers.

### Causality

The study examines the causal relationships among market regimes, including those between the ESI-S&P500 and CSI-BTC, using traditional metrics and entropy-based variants.
The results show that mutual information methods outperform Pearson's correlation in identifying connectivity.
The VAR(1) lag method is also used to test for correlation and causality.

### Sentiment Analysis

The research analyzes the sentiment-returns relationship within and between the stock and cryptocurrency markets.
The results suggest that crypto sentiment and Bitcoin prices co-move, with Bitcoin prices influencing investors' preferences.
Economic sentiments influence Bitcoin dynamics, while 'Crypto' sentiments affect stock market returns.

### Market Influence

The study finds that the two markets influence each other through sentiments spread among investors.
The results imply a 'mediating' role of sentiment in shaping and transmitting investors' beliefs across markets.
The findings are useful for investors and policy-makers, highlighting the linkage and influence of cryptocurrencies on traditional financial and economic instability.

## Builds on previous research

- Shannon [^25] has been introduced in the past to describe financial time series processes, especially to understand the pattern of connections in a globalized world [^2]. Essentially, such methodology is based on the analysis of the uncertainties that different states of the world might generate, and on the different probabilities that different events might occur.

## Differs from previous work

- As reviewed in Scaramozzino et al [^16], information theory dates back to the pioneering work of Shannon [^25] in 1948. From then on, we have waited for different years to observe an application to the financial market contexts [^26].

## Confirmation of earlier findings

- At a glance, it is possible to observe how mutual information methods (always statistically significant) outperform Pearson’s correlation in identifying connectivity. Furthermore, both Correlation and Mutual Information are statistically significant in the Bitcoin relationship with both economic and crypto mood, indicating the instrument's sensitivity and volatility [^20],[^34].
- This is observable from the decrease of TE as q increases. Considering H1, we observe that crypto sentiments and Bitcoin prices co-move, with the latter influencing investors' preferences (similarly to [^35]).

## Limitations

- The study notes that the direct relationship between cryptocurrencies and the stock market has been found to have a scarce connection in previous studies, and that more efforts are required to identify potential linkages with the real economic scenario.
- The limitations of the study include the potential for indirect transmission channels between sentiment metrics and financial returns. The study also notes that the results may be influenced by specific periods in the financial market.

## Future work

- The study suggests that further research is needed to fully understand the relationship between sentiments and price dynamics in the stock and cryptocurrency markets, and to explore the potential applications of entropy-based methods.
- Future work may include identifying and analyzing the sign of the specific indirect transmission channel supposed in the paper. Additionally, future research may examine whether crypto and economic sentiments become more relevant and more closely connected during periods of market distress.
