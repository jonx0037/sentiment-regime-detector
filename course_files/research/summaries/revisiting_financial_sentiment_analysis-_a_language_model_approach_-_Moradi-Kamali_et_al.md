# [Revisiting Financial Sentiment Analysis: A Language Model Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14897v1)

## [[Hamid Moradi-Kamali]]; [[Mohammad-Hossein Rajabi-Ghozlou]]; [[Mahdi Ghazavi]] et al.

## Abstract
Financial Sentiment Analysis (FSA) traditionally relies on human-annotated sentiment labels to infer investor sentiment and forecast market movements. However, inferring the potential market impact of words based on human-perceived intentions is inherently challenging. We hypothesize that the historical market reactions to words offer a more reliable indicator of their potential impact on markets than subjective sentiment interpretations by human annotators. To test this hypothesis, a market-derived labeling approach is proposed to assign tweet labels based on ensuing short-term price trends, enabling the language model to capture the relationship between textual signals and market dynamics directly. A domain-specific language model was fine-tuned on these labels, achieving up to an 11% improvement in short-term trend prediction accuracy over traditional sentiment-based benchmarks. Moreover, by incorporating market and temporal context through prompt tuning, the proposed context-aware language model achieved an accuracy of 89.6% on a curated dataset of 227 impactful Bitcoin-related news events with significant market impact. Aggregating daily tweet predictions into trading signals, our method outperformed traditional fusion models (which combine sentiment-based and price-based predictions). It challenged the assumption that sentiment-based signals are inferior to price-based predictions in forecasting market movements. Backtesting these signals across three distinct market regimes yielded robust Sharpe ratios of up to 5.07 in trending markets and 3.73 in neutral markets. Our findings demonstrate that language models can serve as effective short-term market predictors. This paradigm shift underscores the untapped capabilities of language models in financial decision-making and opens new avenues for market prediction applications.

## Summary

### Introduction
The study revisits Financial Sentiment Analysis (FSA) by proposing a language model approach that focuses on measurable market reactions rather than inferred intentions.
It replaces subjective sentiment labels with market-derived labels that directly reflect ensuing price movements, enabling the model to learn from actual market behavior.

### Methodology
The proposed approach uses a market-derived labeling method called Triple Barrier Labeling (TBL), which provides a robust framework for labeling short-term market trends as Bullish, Bearish, or Neutral.
The study fine-tunes a domain-specific language model on these labels, achieving up to an 11% improvement in short-term trend prediction accuracy over traditional sentiment-based benchmarks.
The model is also enhanced with contextual market data via prompt tuning, enabling it to integrate key market indicators into textual analysis.
The proposed method uses a novel trend-based labeling approach inspired by the TBL method that integrates historical volatility and market-defined barriers to create explainable, actionable labels.
The study uses three datasets: Bitcoin Historical Events, Tweets, and Technical Indicators.
The labeling approach dynamically adjusts upper and lower barriers based on market volatility, using the Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) method to estimate historical volatility.
The study also introduces a Context-UnAware (CUA) model, fine-tuned on market-derived labels, and explores prompt engineering to enhance the model's predictive power.

### Results
The proposed approach demonstrates an accuracy of 89.6% on a curated dataset of 227 impactful Bitcoin-related news events with significant market impacts.
The model's predictions are translated into trading signals and rigorously assessed through backtesting over three years of Bitcoin market data, yielding robust Sharpe ratios of up to 5.07 in trending markets and 3.73 in neutral markets.
The study's findings highlight the potential of language models as effective short-term market predictors, underscoring their untapped capabilities in financial decision-making.

### Background
The study builds on existing research in financial sentiment analysis (FSA), leveraging advances in natural language processing (NLP) and language models such as BERT.
CryptoBERT, a fine-tuned model for cryptocurrency-related social media data, serves as the foundation for this study.
Other models, such as FinEAS and FinGPT, have also demonstrated the potential of NLP for financial forecasting.

### Applications
The study aims to improve short-term trend classification of tweets, using language models to predict market movements.
The approach has the potential to overcome traditional FSA limitations and enhance predictive and actionable utility.
The study's findings can be applied to financial forecasting, risk management, and decision-making, offering exciting opportunities for more accurate, actionable financial predictions.

### Model Development
The Context-Aware (CA) model is an evolution of the CUA model, fine-tuned using enriched prompts that include descriptive terms such as previous label, ROC, and RSI.
The Temporal Context-Aware (TCA) model further refines the CA model by incorporating the tweet date into the prompts, enhancing the model's sensitivity to time-specific market conditions.
The CA model achieves F1-scores of 89.5% on the 2020 dataset and 80.3% on the event-sampled dataset, outperforming the CUA model and baseline models.

### Signal Generation
Two methods for generating signals from tweet aggregation are proposed: the majority vote and the mean methods.
The majority vote method aggregates daily tweet predictions by determining the majority sentiment class for a given day, while the mean method aggregates tweet predictions by averaging the encoded class values over a day.
Both methods provide a daily sentiment signal and its corresponding confidence, which are used in backtesting to evaluate the trading strategy's performance.
The signal generation process translates tweet classifications into actionable trading signals, providing a clear recommendation on the short-term market movement.
The proposed CA language model integrates market features directly into tweet prompts as textual descriptions, allowing for an end-to-end prediction process that inherently models interactions between textual and market data.
The results indicate that the CA model achieved performance comparable to or superior to that of the fusion and price-based models.

### Backtesting And Evaluation
The proposed trading strategies are evaluated through backtesting across various market conditions, using key performance metrics such as cumulative return, daily return, maximum drawdown, and volatility.
Backtesting is conducted using the vectorbt library, and the results are compared with a baseline buy-and-hold strategy.
The evaluation highlights the strengths and weaknesses of each strategy relative to the buy-and-hold baseline, providing insights into the applicability of sentiment-driven trading signals in diverse market environments.

### Model Evaluation
The model's performance is evaluated through extensive SHAP value analysis, which reveals a wide variety of contributions from different parts of the tweets.
This demonstrates the model's ability to adapt to varying linguistic patterns and contexts.
The analysis confirms that the model effectively processes tweet content, ensuring predictions are based on meaningful contextual analysis rather than a superficial reliance on the prompt.

### Trading Strategy
The proposed signal generation methods are applied to three trading strategies: In-Out-Long, In-Out-Short, and TBL.
The results highlight several notable patterns, with the TBL strategies emerging as the top performers, delivering higher Sharpe and Sortino ratios, and demonstrating stable performance across different regimes.
The Majority TBL strategy achieved the highest Sharpe ratio in both the Bullish and Bearish markets, while the Mean TBL method outperformed the others in the stagnant market regime.

### Limitations
The study's approach assumes a direct short-term market impact for each tweet, which may oversimplify the relationship between social media activity and market movements.
The association between specific textual patterns and market trends may be transient, requiring frequent model updates.
The reliance on prompt engineering introduces the risk of overfitting, and reducing numerical features to categorical prompts may lose some fine-grained details.

### Implications
The study provides insights into applying sentiment analysis to financial market forecasting, highlighting the need for more context-aware, market-focused approaches.
The results show that predicting market movements rather than merely assessing sentiment requires a market-derived labeling approach.
Integrating market context through prompt tuning is a pivotal factor in improving predictive performance.

### Future Work
The study demonstrates the potential of language models in financial forecasting, and future research could focus on leveraging them to power chatbots for investment advisory services, advancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation, and improving prompt tuning and feature engineering.
Additionally, personalizing financial advice by tailoring recommendations to individual profiles, risk tolerance, and goals could transform how financial planning is approached, but robust evaluation frameworks are needed to fully assess the models' real-world applicability.

##  Builds on previous research
- On the other hand, removing RSI and ROC results in accuracies of 79.0% and 78.8%, respectively, indicating that these features, while less critical than the previous label, still contribute substantially to model performance. To confirm that the model is not overly reliant on prompts and can effectively interpret tweet content, we conducted an extensive SHAP value analysis [^35] on a diverse range of tweets.
- Traditional sentiment analysis often fails to bridge the gap between textual sentiment and actual market behavior, as evidenced by the poor performance of the CryptoBERT [^15] and FinBERT [^14] models, which achieved F1-scores of only 25.2% and 16.7%, respectively, on a dataset of historical Bitcoin events. In contrast, by using market-derived labeling—defining clear profit/loss and temporal boundaries—we establish a direct link between text and market outcomes.

## Differs from previous work
- While the influence of social media sentiment on financial markets often extends up to 15 days, many studies oversimplify this relationship by relying on single-day thresholds [^6]. However, our approach takes a fundamentally different path by directly using market-derived labels to classify tweets, bypassing the subjectivity of human annotations.
- Recognizing the importance of temporal context in market analysis, we further refine our model by incorporating the date of each tweet into the prompts. Language models typically lack inherent temporal awareness due to their non-sequential processing [^28], [^32].

##  Confirmation of earlier findings
- The results highlight the effectiveness of tailoring labeling methods to align with the specific objectives of FSA, emphasizing that predicting market movements rather than merely assessing sentiment requires a market-derived labeling approach. This finding aligns with previous research [^13] and reinforces the paradigm shift toward directly linking textual content to market behavior [^2], bypassing the limitations of intermediate sentiment evaluations.
