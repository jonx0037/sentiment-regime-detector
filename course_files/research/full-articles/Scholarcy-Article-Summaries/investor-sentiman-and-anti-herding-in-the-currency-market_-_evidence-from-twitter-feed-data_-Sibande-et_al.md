# [Investor Sentiment and (Anti) Herding in the Currency Market: Evidence from Twitter Feed Data](https://doi.org/10.1080/15427560.2021.1917579)

## [[Xolani Sibande]]; [[Rangan Gupta]]; [[Rıza Demirer]] et al.

## Abstract
This paper establishes a direct link between (anti) herding behavior in currency markets and investor sentiment, proxied by a social media based investor happiness index built on Twitter feed data. Our analysis of daily data for nine developed market currencies suggests that the foreign exchange market is generally characterized by strong anti-herding behavior. ==Utilizing the quantile-on-quantile (QQ) approach, developed by Sim and Zhou (Citation2015), we show that the relationship between investor sentiment and anti-herding is in fact regime specific, with anti-herding behavior particularly prominent during states of extreme investor sentiment==. The effect of sentiment on anti-herding is generally stronger in extreme bullish sentiment states, while average sentiment is associated with less severe anti-herding. The findings lend support to the behavioral factors for asset pricing models and suggest that real time investor sentiment signals can be utilized to monitor potential speculative activities in the currency market.

## Summary

### Herding Behavior
The study examines herding behavior in the currency market, with a focus on the relationship between investor sentiment and anti-herding behavior.
The analysis suggests that the foreign exchange market is generally characterized by strong anti-herding behavior, with anti-herding behavior particularly prominent during states of extreme investor sentiment.
The effect of sentiment on anti-herding is stronger in extreme bullish sentiment states, while average sentiment is associated with less severe anti-herding.
The quadratic herding model provides evidence of anti-herding behavior in the currency market, consistently across both the CSAD and CSSD measures.
The rolling window analysis confirms this finding, with significant anti-herding tendencies observed during the financial crisis period and post-2015.
The static analysis reveals a negative relationship between investor happiness and anti-herding, suggesting that positive investor sentiment tends to ease anti-herding.

### Investor Sentiment
The study uses a social media-based investor happiness index built on Twitter feed data to proxy investor sentiment.
The findings suggest that real-time investor sentiment signals can be utilized to monitor potential speculative activities in the currency market.
The literature review highlights the growing evidence that links investor sentiment to anomalies and excess returns in financial markets, as well as herding and speculative behavior.

### Currency Market
The study is motivated by the fact that the foreign exchange market is the largest and most liquid financial market globally, with multiple times the daily volume of international trade flows and stock market transactions executed daily.
The importance of studying herding in foreign exchange markets is illuminated by the evidence of price cascades in currency markets due to stop-loss orders, which can be driven by informational cascades.
The study adds to the literature on herding in financial markets by establishing a direct link between (anti) herding behavior in currency markets and investor sentiment.

### Introduction
The study explores the relationship between investor sentiment and herding behavior in the currency market.
Investor sentiment is measured using the investor happiness index, which is constructed from Twitter user-generated data.
The study examines the role of sentiment as a determinant of herding or anti-herding behavior in the currency market.

### Methodology
The study uses a sample of nine developed market currencies and calculates two dispersion metrics: cross-sectional standard deviation (CSSD) and cross-sectional absolute standard deviations (CSAD).
The study also estimates herding coefficients using OLS with robust standard errors and implements one-day rolling regressions with 250-day and 500-day windows.
Additionally, the study uses quantile regression to capture the dynamic dependencies in the data.

### Results
The study presents descriptive statistics, including daily mean and standard deviation, skewness and kurtosis estimates, and normality tests.
The results of the herding tests and the relationship between herding coefficients and the happiness index are also presented.
However, the specific results are not detailed in the provided text, and the study appears to be setting up the methodology and framework for analyzing the relationship between investor sentiment and herding behavior in the currency market.

### Data Characteristics
The data exhibits leptokurtic distributions with high kurtosis values and mixed skewness estimates, indicating that the series are not normally distributed.
The ARCH-LM and Ljung-Box tests reveal the presence of heteroskedasticity and serial correlation in the data.
The unconditional correlations between currency pairs range from -0.83 to 0.82, with some pairs showing low correlation.

### Sentiment And Herding
The quantile-on-quantile regression analysis shows that the relationship between investor sentiment and anti-herding is regime-specific.
During periods of extreme bullish or bearish sentiment, anti-herding behavior is stronger, while average sentiment is associated with less severe anti-herding.
The findings suggest that real-time investor sentiment signals can be used to monitor potential speculative activities in the currency market.

## Differs from previous work
- During periods of extreme bullish periods, we observe a positive effect of happiness, which means extreme bullish sentiment generally makes anti-herding even stronger. ==This can also be seen during extreme bearish sentiment when the effect becomes strongly positive. [^Daviou_2014_a]) argue that investors do not necessarily lose confidence during extreme increases in risk and instead, they argue, investors build confidence over sharp, subsequent declines in risk==.

##  Confirmation of earlier findings
- The effect of sentiment on anti-herding is generally stronger in extreme bullish sentiment states, while average sentiment is associated with less severe anti herding. While ==the findings are generally in line with the evidence in the literature== (e.g., [^Gavriilidis_et+al_2016_a]; [^Blasco_et+al_2018_a]) that links herding behavior to extreme events, they also lend support to the behavioral factors for asset pricing models and suggest that real time investor sentiment signals can be utilized to monitor potential speculative activities in the currency market.

## Counterpoint to earlier claims
- The possible presence of herding behavior in the currency market is further motivated by the evidence of commonalities in currency traders’ risk preferences (e.g., [^Dominguez_1993_a]; Carlson 1998; Bensaid and De Bandt 2000; [^Mende_et+al_2004_a]; [^Osler_2005_a]; Bjønnes and Rime 2005) and the recent evidence that links the time- variation in risk preferences to currency excess returns ([^Demirer_2021_a]) and the profitability of carry trades ([^Demirer_et+al_2020_a]). ==Interestingly, however, although several studies were conducted to examine herding in the foreign exchange market, they remain limited in volume and the utilization of the empirical measures to detect herding== (see [^Kim_et+al_2004_a]; [^Park_2011_a]; [^Pierdzioch_et+al_2012_a]; and others).

## Limitations
- The study notes that the literature on herding in the currency market remains limited in volume and the utilization of empirical measures to detect herding. The study also acknowledges that the drivers of herding in foreign exchange markets remain a subject of debate.
- The study notes that standard quantile regressions are limited in their ability to capture dependency in its entirety. The study also notes that financial markets are often characterized by regime-dependent patterns for normal, bullish and bearish market states.
- The study does not explicitly discuss its limitations, but it notes that the choice of bandwidth in kernel regression remains uncertain. The study also uses a specific investor sentiment index based on Twitter feed data, which may not be representative of all investors.

## Future work
- The study suggests that future research could explore the relationship between herding and investor sentiment in other financial markets, such as equity markets. The study also highlights the importance of considering the dynamic nature of herding behavior in the currency market.
- Future work can build on the study's results by examining how real-time sentiment proxies drive intraday trades in these markets.
